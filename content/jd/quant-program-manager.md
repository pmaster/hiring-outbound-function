# Technical Program Manager

Sit between the execution floor and engineering, and hold both to an error rate.

## Who we are

We are a small trading firm in alternative assets. Around fifty people. Our
execution work is high volume, detailed, and done by people following playbooks
against software that other companies wrote badly. When it goes wrong it costs
real money on the day, not in a quarterly report.

## The job

- Own the execution numbers. Output per person per week, coverage per client,
  logging error rate, execution error rate. All of them are measured today and
  most of them are worse than they should be.
- Cut the low leverage work off the head of the desk. Target is 75 percent.
- Translate between the floor and engineering. You write the requirements that
  make the tooling fit the work, rather than the other way round.
- Hold the line on service levels and error rates, and stay flexible on how
  people get there.

## You

- A high accountability leader. You have the direct conversation when a number
  is missed, and you have it early.
- Process obsessed. You think in checklists, standard procedures and error
  rates.
- Product minded. You have written requirements that an engineer could build
  from without a meeting.
- A data hawk. Comfortable in spreadsheets and able to write a basic query.

The backgrounds that fit: IT project managers, technical implementation leads,
and operations managers from trading firms, payments, or high volume
fulfilment.

## What we are bad at, stated up front

- Nothing logs errors anywhere in the business.
- Output per person varies by a factor of seven across the same role and nobody
  has fixed it.
- Dashboards exist and nobody trusts the numbers on them. That was a
  definitions failure, not a visualisation failure.
