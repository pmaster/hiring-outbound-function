# Chief of Staff

Take whole problems off the founder's desk and give them back finished.

## Who we are

We are a small trading firm in alternative assets. Around fifty people. We
have been profitable, we have been unprofitable, and we are rebuilding the
operating side from close to first principles this quarter.

## Why this seat exists

The founder is the bottleneck on roughly a third of everything happening in
the company. Not because he took on too much by choice, but because three
senior seats do not exist and the work landed on him anyway.

We keep a list of every open piece of work in the business. About sixty items
on it have nobody's name against them, and fifteen of those are large.
"Nobody" is the largest single carrier of heavy work here. That is the honest
description of this seat.

## The job

This is not an executive assistant role and it is not a calendar job. You are
a proxy. You take a problem, own it end to end, and hand it to whoever should
run it.

Four that are open right now, all of them written down months ago and none of
them owned:

- **The two leaks in the funnel.** Roughly half the people who book a session
  never attend it. Of the people who accept an offer, about a quarter finish
  onboarding. Both are solvable with scheduling design, better communication
  and some tooling. Neither has an owner.
- **Work nobody has timed.** Several teams have no defensible view of how long
  their own work should take. You run the time studies, set the quotas, and
  hold them.
- **A support function that does not exist.** You write the playbook, define
  the tiers, and get it staffed.
- **Special projects.** New market launches, new initiatives, senior hiring.
  Several per quarter, from idea to handed off.

And the fifth thing, which is half the job: **saying no on the founder's
behalf**, including to the founder. You need to be able to do that and keep
the relationship.

## You

- Two or more years in a chief of staff, strategy and operations, consulting,
  banking or startup generalist role, in something fast moving.
- You have operated, not only advised. Something existed when you left that
  did not exist when you arrived. This is the whole test.
- A ruthless prioritiser. You will let three small fires burn to put out the
  one that actually costs money.
- A clear, concise writer who can represent someone else's voice.
- Tech-savvy and resourceful. You can write a requirements brief an engineer
  can build from.
- High emotional intelligence, real discretion, and tact.
- Comfortable with ambiguity, because there is a great deal of it.

Having founded something yourself, whether or not it worked, counts for a lot
here.

## What we are bad at, stated up front

- We have no financial reporting worth the name.
- Error rates are unmeasured in three departments.
- Most of our systems run through one engineer.
- Almost nothing is written down.
- Four specific, quantified improvements were identified six months ago,
  written down clearly, and are all still open. Nobody was assigned.

If that reads as a warning, this is not your role. If it reads as a list you
could fix in six months, we should talk.

## What you get

- Unusually broad exposure: operations, finance, hiring, expansion, product.
- A founder who has already written down what he thinks is wrong.
- Fast decisions. There is no committee.
- A genuine path into a functional leadership seat as the company grows.

## How the process runs

Written questions first, before any call. Then a timed exercise: six problems
that belong to nobody, and you decide which you own, which you hand over and
to whom, and which you let burn. Then an open ended plan for standing up a
function from zero, which we pay for.

We do it this way because our own hiring records say the interview is the
worst predictor we have, and prior work history is the best.
