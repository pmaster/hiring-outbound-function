# Controller

Build the finance function. There has never been an accounting pass.

## Who we are

We are a small trading firm in alternative assets. Around fifty people. We
have been profitable, we have been unprofitable, and we are rebuilding the
operating side from close to first principles this quarter. Revenue went from
roughly $150k a month in 2025 to roughly $1m a month by the middle of 2026,
and the finance function did not grow with it.

## Why this seat exists

Eighteen open pieces of work sit on this seat. Eleven of them are on the
founder today because there is nobody else. It is the heaviest vacancy in the
company and it is the reason several other things are not moving.

The company has never had an accounting pass. Not a late one, not a rough one.
None. Nobody can state assets, runway, or a forward plan from the books,
because the books were built to record what was spent rather than to support a
decision.

## The job

- **Do the accounting pass that has never been done.** Assets, liabilities,
  runway, a forward plan. Everything downstream of it is currently guesswork.
- **Make the money visible.** Funds sit across more than a hundred accounts.
  Nobody can state the total on any given day. Finance reconstructs it by hand
  once a month and the founder describes this as the thing that worries him
  most.
- **Own the entity architecture.** Which entity issues money and which
  collects it. Intercompany agreements at arm's length, own bank accounts, own
  books. This is on the critical path for a market reopening, and the critical
  path is entity, then tax registration, then bank. Not the website.
- **Choose the payment rails.** Check each provider's acceptable use policy
  against what the business actually does, before committing. Careful wording
  does not fix a policy conflict, and finding that out after onboarding is
  expensive.
- **Set float and recall policy.** Buffer size per entity, recall cadence for
  excess. This exists so that a gap between one settlement and the next never
  tempts an improvisation.
- **Measure the losses.** Three categories of loss are real, recurring and
  entirely unmeasured. All the unit economics we quote today are pre-loss.
- **Produce a daily spend and revenue readout.** A decision aid, not a report.
  Nothing runs daily today.

## You

- You have been the first finance hire, or close to it. Something existed when
  you left that did not exist when you arrived.
- You are comfortable with multi-entity structures, intercompany agreements
  and cross-border movement of money. This is most of the job.
- You have worked somewhere with a large contractor or marketplace payout
  volume, or you can show the equivalent.
- You are a strong, direct writer. You will explain a structure to a
  non-financial founder who will then act on it.
- You are willing to say no to the founder about money, in writing, and hold
  the line afterwards. We will ask for the occasion.
- You handle sensitive financial arrangements with discretion and care.

Audit-only backgrounds are a poor fit. Not a rejection, but the operating seat
is the experience that counts here.

## What we are bad at, stated up front

- The books tell you what was spent, not what to decide.
- There is no daily view of cash anywhere in the business.
- Three categories of loss are unmeasured, so every margin figure we have is
  optimistic by an unknown amount.
- Two documents currently in use are known to be wrong and are still being
  used for planning.
- Most of our systems run through one engineer, and the finance data is
  downstream of them.

If that reads as a warning, this is not your role. If it reads as a list you
could fix in six months, we should talk.

## What you get

- A finance function to build, not one to maintain.
- Real revenue underneath you, and a genuine mess on top of it.
- A founder who has written down what he thinks is wrong, so you are not
  reverse engineering the problem from meetings.
- Fast decisions. There is no committee, and no board to educate.
- Direct work with outside tax and legal counsel from week one.

## How the process runs

Written questions first, before any call. Then a timed simulation of the
actual work: a set of accounts that do not tie out, and a founder who needs
the month-end note in plain English. Then an open ended exercise on a real
structural question this business has, which we pay for.

We do it this way because our own hiring records say the interview is the
worst predictor we have, and prior work history is the best. You will be
judged on what you produce.
