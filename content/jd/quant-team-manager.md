# Quantitative Team Manager

Run an execution floor where accuracy matters as much as speed.

## Who we are

We are a small trading firm in alternative assets. Around fifty people. Our
strategies are identified by a small research team and then executed by an
operations floor working through structured playbooks. The edge is real.
Capturing it depends entirely on execution being accurate, fast and
consistent.

## Why this seat exists

The person who runs the floor is also the person who does the research. So the
research is not happening, and the floor is not managed as well as it should
be. You take the floor. He takes back the work only he can do.

## The job

- **Raise output per person.** The spread between the best and the weakest
  operator is a factor of several. That gap is the number you are attacking,
  through training, better process, clearer standards and daily oversight.
- **Drive error rates down and keep them down.** When someone makes a mistake,
  the first question is what about the system allowed it. The second is what
  changes so it cannot happen again. Nothing currently logs errors at all, so
  you build that too.
- **Own training and onboarding for the floor.** Ramp time is currently
  measured in months and none of it is written down. Systematise it, document
  it, then delegate it.
- **Be the bridge to engineering.** You will spend real time watching people
  work, spotting what should not be manual, and writing prioritised
  requirements that get built.
- **Keep the hopper full.** Nobody should sit idle waiting on equipment, a
  handoff or an answer.
- **Take the escalations.** Anything the playbook does not cover comes to you.

## You

- You have managed a high volume execution, production or fulfilment floor
  where accuracy mattered as much as speed.
- You think in checklists, standard operating procedures and error rates. You
  believe a mistake is a failure of the system first and the person second.
- You are highly literate with spreadsheets and comfortable with basic data
  querying. You spot patterns individuals working alone would not.
- You write clear technical requirements. You can describe a broken workflow
  precisely enough that someone can build the fix.
- You are uncompromising on standards and genuinely good with early-career
  people. Both, not one.
- You are rigid about hitting targets and flexible about how.

**You do not need to be a mathematician.** This is an operations job with
probability in it, not a maths job. A strong quantitative background is
welcome and it is not what we select on.

## Backgrounds that fit

Wider than the title suggests, which is why we are writing to people who would
never search for it:

- Trading, exchange or brokerage operations. Middle office, trade support.
- High volume production, fulfilment or field operations.
- Casino, sportsbook or iGaming operations.
- Technical program or project management, especially where you wrote the
  specifications engineering built from.
- Quality assurance or process improvement leadership.

A competitive background in poker, chess, esports or trading is a genuine
plus. Our own hiring record says real interest in the domain predicts effort
better than almost anything else we measure.

## What we are bad at, stated up front

- Nothing logs errors anywhere in the business.
- Ramp time is months and no training is written down.
- No time study has ever been run on this floor, so every productivity number
  we quote is an estimate.
- Most of our systems run through one engineer, so the automation queue is
  long.

- Direct access to the founder and the head of research, with no layers.
- A function that is the profit centre of the company, owned outright.
- A real growth path as the floor expands into new regions.

## How the process runs

Written questions first, before any call. Then a timed exercise: a floor where
output varies wildly and an error log with a pattern in it. You find the
pattern, say whether it is a person problem or a system problem and how you
know, and write the specification for the fix. Then a live written exchange
that tests how you handle a counterparty who says no.

We do it this way because our own hiring records say the interview is the
worst predictor we have, and prior work history is the best.
